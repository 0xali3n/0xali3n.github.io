From hackathon prototype to product discipline

Chinmay Patil on what transfers from hackathon building into real product work — drawing on Anthé Labs and competition experience.

By Chinmay Patil · Based on documented product and hackathon work

Hackathons compress product discovery into a weekend. That compression is useful: you learn which ideas survive contact with a demo, a judge panel, or a live chain. It is also misleading if you treat the demo as the product.

When I look at work such as Anthé Labs — which began in hackathon prototyping and later required co-founder-level product and engineering ownership — the transferable skills are specific:

  • Scope control. A weekend forces a thin vertical slice. Product work keeps that habit but extends the slice carefully instead of rewriting everything.
  • Integration realism. Copy trading, explorers, and agent wallets fail at boundaries: RPC quirks, wallet UX, indexing lag. Hackathons expose those boundaries early.
  • Team communication under time. Five-person startup coordination is slower than a hackathon huddle, but the same clarity about owners and interfaces still matters.

What does not transfer automatically is durability: monitoring, onboarding, support paths, and the unglamorous work of making a feature boringly reliable. Competition prizes validate speed and inventiveness; they do not replace product discipline.

I still enter hackathons because they are high-signal learning environments. I treat them as laboratories for ideas that may later deserve the slower craft documented across this site’s projects and experience pages.
